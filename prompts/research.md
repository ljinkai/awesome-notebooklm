# Research Prompts

Prompts for literature review, argument analysis, gap identification, and research synthesis. These assume you have uploaded academic papers, reports, or other research documents as sources.

---

## Argument & Claim Analysis

### Core Argument Extractor
**Use case:** When you need to quickly understand what each source is actually arguing, beyond the abstract.
**Prompt:**
> For each source in this notebook, identify: (1) the central claim or thesis, (2) the primary evidence used to support it, (3) the methodology, and (4) the main limitations the authors themselves acknowledge. Present this as a structured table with one row per source.

**Tips:** Follow up with "Which sources make the most similar central claims?" to identify clusters.

---

### Assumption Auditor
**Use case:** Identifying unstated assumptions in research — useful before building on someone else's framework.
**Prompt:**
> Examine the uploaded sources and identify the key assumptions each author makes that are not explicitly stated or defended. For each assumption, note: (1) which source makes it, (2) what the assumption is, (3) whether any other source challenges or contradicts this assumption, and (4) what would need to be true for the assumption to hold.

**Tips:** Works especially well when you have both empirical papers and theoretical frameworks in the same notebook.

---

### Counter-Argument Generator
**Use case:** Stress-testing your own argument or preparing for peer review.
**Prompt:**
> Based on the sources in this notebook, generate the strongest possible counter-arguments to the following claim: [paste your claim here]. For each counter-argument, cite the specific source that supports it and explain the logical chain from the source to the counter-argument. Do not invent counter-arguments that are not grounded in the uploaded material.

---

### Competing Explanations Mapper
**Use case:** When multiple sources explain the same phenomenon differently.
**Prompt:**
> The sources in this notebook offer different explanations for [phenomenon]. Map out the competing explanations: (1) list each distinct explanation, (2) identify which sources support each explanation, (3) describe what empirical evidence would distinguish between them, and (4) note where the sources themselves engage with or dismiss competing views.

## Literature & Gap Analysis

### Literature Gap Finder
**Use case:** Identifying what has not been studied — essential for framing original contributions.
**Prompt:**
> Based on the uploaded papers, identify gaps in the existing literature. Specifically look for: (1) populations or contexts that are understudied, (2) questions the authors themselves flag as unanswered, (3) methodological limitations that prevent current studies from answering certain questions, (4) contradictions between studies that remain unresolved, and (5) assumptions shared across all sources that have not been tested.

---

### Research Frontier Mapper
**Use case:** Understanding where a field is heading, not just where it has been.
**Prompt:**
> Looking across all the uploaded sources, what are the emerging questions and open problems that researchers in this field are moving toward? What methods are being introduced or proposed? What theoretical frameworks are gaining traction? Present this as a "state of the field" summary with forward-looking emphasis.

---

### Methodology Comparison
**Use case:** Evaluating which research designs are most appropriate for a question you are investigating.
**Prompt:**
> Compare the research methodologies used across the uploaded sources. For each study, identify: the research design, sample or data source, key measures or variables, analytical approach, and notable methodological strengths and weaknesses. Then summarize: which methodological approaches appear most rigorous given the research questions, and what methodological innovations (if any) appear in recent papers compared to older ones?

---

### Consensus vs. Debate Mapper
**Use case:** Distinguishing settled questions from live debates in a literature.
**Prompt:**
> Based on the uploaded sources, distinguish between: (1) claims that appear to have broad consensus across multiple sources, (2) claims that are actively debated with credible arguments on multiple sides, and (3) claims made by only one or two sources that have not been widely tested. For each category, list the specific claims and the sources that address them.

## Synthesis & Summarization

### Structured Literature Review Generator
**Use case:** Generating a first draft of a literature review section.
**Prompt:**
> Write a structured literature review based on the uploaded sources. Organize it thematically rather than source-by-source. For each theme: (1) state what the literature agrees on, (2) note significant disagreements, (3) describe how understanding has evolved over time if the sources span different years, and (4) identify what remains unclear. Use inline citations referencing the specific uploaded sources throughout.

---

### Cross-Source Synthesis Table
**Use case:** Quick comparison across many sources on specific dimensions.
**Prompt:**
> Create a synthesis table comparing all uploaded sources across the following dimensions: research question, theoretical framework, methodology, key findings, and implications for practice. Format as a markdown table with one row per source. Where a source does not address a dimension, mark it as "not addressed" rather than leaving it blank.

---

### Key Concept Mapper
**Use case:** Understanding how technical terms and concepts are used across a literature — definitions often vary.
**Prompt:**
> Identify the 8–12 most important concepts or terms used across the uploaded sources. For each concept: (1) provide the definition each source uses (noting differences), (2) explain how central the concept is to each source's argument, (3) note any conceptual evolution or disagreement across sources. Present this as a concept glossary with source-specific notes.

---

### Theoretical Framework Extractor
**Use case:** Understanding the underlying theory before engaging with empirical claims.
**Prompt:**
> For each uploaded source, identify the theoretical framework or model that underpins the research. Describe: (1) the core assumptions of the framework, (2) what it predicts or explains, (3) what it leaves out or cannot explain, and (4) how it relates to the frameworks in other uploaded sources. Are the sources building from compatible foundations, or do they rest on incompatible theoretical assumptions?

## Critical Analysis

### Evidence Quality Evaluator
**Use case:** Assessing how well conclusions are supported by the evidence in each source.
**Prompt:**
> For each uploaded source, evaluate the quality of evidence used to support the main claims. Consider: sample size and representativeness, whether the study design allows causal inference or only correlation, how well the authors control for confounds, whether the findings have been replicated, and whether the conclusions match the scope of the evidence. Rate each source as strong, moderate, or weak evidence for its central claim, and explain your rating.

---

### Replication and Generalizability Audit
**Use case:** Before applying findings to a new context, assess whether the evidence travels.
**Prompt:**
> For the main findings across the uploaded sources, assess: (1) which findings have been replicated across multiple independent studies, (2) which are based on a single study or single context, (3) what populations or settings the research was conducted in, and (4) what would need to be true for the findings to generalize to [your target context]. Flag any findings that should be treated as preliminary given limited replication.

---

### Bias and Limitation Checker
**Use case:** Preparing a rigorous critique or strengthening your own arguments.
**Prompt:**
> Examine each uploaded source for potential sources of bias or systematic limitation. Consider: (1) funding sources and potential conflicts of interest if mentioned, (2) publication bias (do the studies only report positive findings?), (3) measurement limitations, (4) sample selection issues, (5) researcher positionality if acknowledged. Summarize the most significant limitations that affect the overall body of evidence in this notebook.
